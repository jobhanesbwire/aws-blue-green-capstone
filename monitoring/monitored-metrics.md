# Monitored metrics

This document summarizes the AWS metrics that should be reviewed during the deployment.

## Application Load Balancer

### HealthyHostCount
- Why it matters: Confirms that the target group has at least one healthy registered instance.
- Unusual value: A sharp drop may indicate an instance issue or failed health check.
- Deployment impact: This should block or reverse deployment if the Green target group loses healthy capacity.

### UnHealthyHostCount
- Why it matters: Confirms the number of unhealthy targets behind the ALB.
- Unusual value: A rising count may indicate failing instances or a misconfigured health check.
- Deployment impact: This should block or reverse deployment when Green becomes unhealthy.

### RequestCount
- Why it matters: Measures the volume of requests reaching the target groups.
- Unusual value: A sudden drop or spike may point to routing or availability concerns.
- Deployment impact: This should be correlated with other metrics before changing weights.

### HTTPCode_Target_5XX_Count
- Why it matters: Shows server-side errors returned by the targets.
- Unusual value: Elevated counts may indicate application regressions.
- Deployment impact: This should block or reverse deployment if errors rise above the agreed threshold.

### HTTPCode_ELB_5XX_Count
- Why it matters: Shows errors generated by the ALB itself.
- Unusual value: A spike may indicate listener or target-group issues.
- Deployment impact: This should trigger investigation and possible rollback.

### TargetResponseTime
- Why it matters: Measures how long targets take to respond.
- Unusual value: A sustained increase may indicate performance degradation.
- Deployment impact: This should slow or reverse the deployment when response times become unacceptable.

### TargetConnectionErrorCount
- Why it matters: Measures connection failures from the ALB to the targets.
- Unusual value: A rise may indicate network or target-group issues.
- Deployment impact: This should cause investigation and possible rollback.

### RejectedConnectionCount
- Why it matters: Tracks rejected connections due to capacity or connection limits.
- Unusual value: High numbers may indicate overload or a target-group problem.
- Deployment impact: This may justify pausing the rollout and reviewing scaling or configuration.

## EC2

### CPUUtilization
- Why it matters: Measures host CPU pressure.
- Unusual value: Sustained high CPU may indicate overload or poor performance.
- Deployment impact: This can support or delay deployment depending on workload baselines.

### NetworkIn
- Why it matters: Tracks incoming network traffic.
- Unusual value: Large changes may reflect traffic shifts or abnormal loads.
- Deployment impact: This should be interpreted with the workload baseline.

### NetworkOut
- Why it matters: Tracks outgoing network traffic.
- Unusual value: Large changes may reflect a traffic surge or abnormal behavior.
- Deployment impact: This should be investigated if it coincides with errors.

### StatusCheckFailed
- Why it matters: Indicates a failed EC2 status check.
- Unusual value: A failing status check typically means the instance is unhealthy.
- Deployment impact: This should block or reverse deployment until the issue is understood.

### StatusCheckFailed_Instance
- Why it matters: Indicates a failed instance-level status check.
- Unusual value: This may represent an internal instance problem.
- Deployment impact: This should trigger investigation and may require rollback.

### StatusCheckFailed_System
- Why it matters: Indicates a failed system-level status check.
- Unusual value: This may indicate hardware or platform health issues.
- Deployment impact: This should be treated as a high-severity signal for the operations team.
